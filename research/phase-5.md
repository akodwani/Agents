# Phase 5 — Adversarial pass

**Research date:** 2026-08-17

Attacking the strategy emerging from Phases 1–4. Everything below either kills a recommendation, downgrades it, or survives.

---

## Attack 1: "The agency/holdco target is contracting — you're advising him to swim upstream"

**This attack lands. Fully.**
- WPP: hundreds more cuts globally through end of 2026 ([Ad Age](https://adage.com/agencies/aa-wpp-plans-hundreds-more-layoffs-by-end-of-2026/)).
- Omnicom: 4,000 post-IPG-merger cuts on top of 8,200+ pre-merger; savings target doubled to $1.5B with **$1B from labor** ([FT via MarketScreener](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/omnicom-to-cut-4-000-jobs-shut-several-agencies-after-ipg-takeover-ft-reports-ce7d51d8db8ff527)).
- 150,000+ job cuts citing AI since early 2025; **91% of senior US agency leaders expect AI to reduce headcount** ([AI CERTs](https://www.aicerts.ai/news/marketing-agency-layoffs-surge-as-wpp-pursues-ai-efficiency-drive/)).
- Explicit: holdcos are "largely shifting focus to enhancing AI capabilities rather than employing new and emerging talent."
- US entry-level hiring −6% YoY (Dec 2025–Feb 2026); creative absent from NACE's top entry-level-hiring sectors for the Class of 2026.

**Adjustment: holdcos are removed from the 90-day target list entirely.** Not deprioritized — removed. Targeting shifts to independents standing up AI video units (Collier Simon pattern), small AI-native shops, and freelance. WPP Hex stays as a once-a-year lottery ticket, correctly priced at zero expected value.

**Note the cruel irony this exposes:** the same force creating "AI creative" job titles is destroying the junior rungs that would have trained him. Both things are true. Do not let the growth narrative obscure the headcount narrative.

## Attack 2: "Liu Ziyu and Zack London are survivorship bias"

**This attack lands, and it is the most important one in the report.**

Millions of people post AI video. I found **two** who converted attention into a career event, and they are famous *because* they are rare — that is literally why they surfaced in a search. Building a plan on "make something that goes viral" is building a plan on winning a lottery, and any report that does that is malpractice.

**But the attack does not kill the channel — it corrects the target.** The load-bearing case is not Liu Ziyu. It is **Victor Moreno, hired off a Nike spec ad with ~2,000 views in its first week.** Two thousand views is not a lottery. Two thousand views is a competent piece of work existing in a findable place.

**Adjustment:** the strategy's success criterion is explicitly **NOT reach**. Public posting is reframed from "distribution strategy" to "**making the work legible and findable when a specific named human looks him up.**" Any plan step whose success depends on an algorithm is cut. Viral outcomes are treated as unbudgeted upside.

## Attack 3: "Freelance AI video is a race to the bottom — +329% demand attracts +400% supply"

**Partially lands.**
- Real evidence of the floor: a genuine Upwork posting at **$20 fixed price** for a Higgsfield+ComfyUI Instagram video (2026-02-10). Full-time ComfyUI roles listed at **$15–$21/hr**. The commoditized tier is real and it pays nothing.
- Barriers to entry are near zero and falling — that's the entire premise of the tools.

**What survives:** the demand signal is *client spending*, not sentiment, and it is the strongest single data point in this report (+329% YoY, fastest-growing category on Upwork). The $150–$1,200/deliverable and $1,500–$8,000/mo retainer tiers exist above the floor. Industry commentary converges on the differentiator being **taste, judgment, and commercial context** rather than generation ability — because generation is the commoditized part.

**Adjustment — and this is where his profile stops being a liability:** he must not compete on price or on volume in the commodity tier. His wedge is that he is a **DIRECTV ad-sales revenue/expense operator who can also make the asset**. He can read a campaign P&L, talk CTV pricing, and explain why a creative variant underperformed. Essentially no AI artist in the $20-per-video pool can do that. Positioning is **"performance creative for advertisers,"** not "AI video guy." Same output, different buyer, ~10x the price.

## Attack 4: "The junior AI-creative role is being automated away — you're training him for a job that won't exist"

**Does not land, in the 90-day frame; unresolved beyond it.**
- Market is growing: AI in media/entertainment $28.32B (2025) → $35.77B (2026), 26.3% CAGR; generative AI in creative work a $15.6B market in 2026.
- Job titles that literally read "AI Artist (Creative & Image Generation)" are being posted at scale by Brandtech+.
- Unilever's Beauty AI Studio is live across **18 markets** producing real assets — that work is being done by employed people.

**The real risk is not that the work disappears — it's that the work exists but the seat is offshore.** Brandtech+'s 100 roles hub in South Africa (220+ local staff, a "1,000-person creative engine") is the tell: high-volume AI production is being concentrated in lower-cost hubs. Combined with mid-to-senior-only requirements, the NYC junior seat is genuinely scarce.

**Adjustment:** none to the 90-day plan, but the honest framing is that **freelance/contract is not a stepping stone to a full-time seat that is reliably waiting at the end.** It may be the destination. Plan accordingly — build a client base, not just a resume.

## Attack 5: "The community channel is thin and you know it"

**Lands.** I found no direct evidence of gig flow transacting inside Higgsfield's or Curious Refuge's Discords, and could verify nothing at all about Banodoco.

**Adjustment:** community hours cut hard. What survives is only the *specific* mechanisms with traceable outcomes: the Curious Refuge **jobs board**, its **studio partnerships** (including a formal Promise relationship), its **NYC in-person meetups**, and Higgsfield's **CPP direct line to the creator team**. Passive Discord presence is cut from the plan entirely.

---

## Attack 6: "Cold-outreach reply rates are borrowed from B2B sales and don't transfer"

**Lands.** 15–35% X DM reply rates come from sales-tooling vendors with an interest in the number being high, measuring a different activity with a different ask.

**Adjustment:** outreach reply-rate expectations are cut to a fraction of the cited figures in the final plan, and outreach is repositioned from a primary channel to a **multiplier on the spec work** — the thing that makes a finished piece land in a specific inbox. It is never the first move.

---

## Attack 7: "ComfyUI in 90 days while working full-time is unrealistic — you're prescribing a tool detour"

**Partially lands.** He is time-boxed to ~10 hrs/week with a draining day job. Learning ComfyUI to professional depth is not a two-week project, and every hour on it is an hour not spent making the piece that actually gets him seen.

**But the evidence for it is specific and hard to dismiss:** ComfyUI was the *stated deciding factor* in the one clean junior-hire case in the entire sample (Victor Moreno → UNCUT, "instrumental… due to the company's highly technical profile"), and it appears in essentially every posted tool stack.

**Adjustment — sequencing, not deletion:** the finished spec piece ships **first**, in Higgsfield, which he already knows. ComfyUI is a background thread starting week 4, targeting demonstrable-not-expert by day 90. Deferring it entirely would leave the exact gap that decided the one case we can actually trace. Rushing it would mean 90 days with nothing shipped.

## Attack 8: Stress-testing the 90-day timeline

Realistic time-to-first-outcome per channel, from the evidence:

| Channel | Realistic time to first outcome | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Higgsfield Earn payout | **2–5 weeks** | 90% approval rate, weekly $100k pool, open to any size |
| First paid freelance gig | **4–10 weeks** | Requires portfolio piece + proposals; +329% demand tailwind |
| Freelance retainer ($1.5–8k/mo) | **4–8 months** | Requires repeat-client trust |
| Inbound from public work | **2 weeks – never** | Liu: ~2 weeks (outlier). Victor: unstated. High variance |
| Internal DIRECTV move | **2–6 months** | Internal cycles; but zero applicant competition |
| Full-time creative role, external | **6–18 months** | Referral hires close in ~22 days *once sourced*; non-referral ~37 days — but sourcing is the bottleneck, and reqs want 2–4 yrs |
| Cold applications | **Not a timeline. A coin flip with a bad coin** | ~1/242 |

**Verdict on the 3-month deadline: a full-time creative role in 90 days is not realistic, and the plan must say so.** What *is* realistic in 90 days: **first paid AI-creative work, a portfolio at bar, and 2–4 live conversations with people who hire.** That is a credible exit path in motion, which is what he actually said he needs — he asked for a credible path moving, not a signed offer.

**The single biggest timeline risk is not the market — it is him quitting the day job early.** The DIRECTV job is funding the runway *and* is the warm industry network *and* is the differentiator (Attack 3). Nothing in this evidence supports leaving it inside 90 days.

## What survived

1. Holdcos: **cut.**
2. Viral-as-strategy: **cut**, replaced with findability at Victor Moreno scale (~2K views).
3. Commodity freelance: **cut**, replaced with performance-creative positioning using his ad-ops wedge.
4. Passive community presence: **cut**, replaced with CR jobs board + NYC meetups + Higgsfield CPP.
5. Outreach as a primary channel: **downgraded** to a multiplier on shipped work.
6. ComfyUI: **kept but resequenced** behind the spec piece.
7. Full-time-role-in-90-days: **cut as a goal**, replaced with first-paid-work + portfolio-at-bar + live conversations.
8. **Survived intact and strengthened:** ship one finished, graded, sound-designed spec ad for a real brand; put it on a video platform; use it as the lead artifact in targeted outreach to small shops; monetize immediately via Higgsfield Earn and Upwork; keep the DIRECTV job and mine it.
