## 0. THE HOUR BUDGET — and the finding that reframes every recommendation

The prompt's G2 gate allows a gap of **≤120 buildable hours**. Before applying it, the actual
budget has to be established, because the gate is generous relative to what exists.

**Gross capacity:** 13 weeks × ~10 hrs/week = **~130 hours.** (Acting classes from October sit
outside this as separately scheduled evenings, but they draw on the same finite energy alongside
a draining full-time job — noted as a risk, not billed as hours.)

**Already committed (per the profile, assumed to exist by mid-November):**

| Committed item | Hours [M] | Basis for the estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Learn an NLE from **zero** to graded + sound-designed output | **25–35** | Resolve basics 1–2 weeks of consistent practice; "importing, editing, colour grading, basic sound design" ≈ 1 month; Colour + Fairlight comfort "a few weeks of regular practice" [V, miracamp.com/learn/davinci-resolve/is-it-hard-to-learn-for-beginners ; beginnersapproach.com/davinci-resolve-how-long-to-learn ; davinciresolve21.com/blog/how-long-to-learn] |
| D.R.C.A. 45–60s master cut: generation, iteration, assembly, grade, sound | **25–35** | [M] |
| 30–60s process breakdown | **8–12** | [M] |
| Video-first portfolio domain | **8–12** | [M] — low because he codes |
| Instagram content studio launch + first cadence | **10–15** | [M] |
| First paid external brief: outreach, negotiation, delivery | **15–25** | [M] |
| **Committed subtotal** | **91–134** | |

**Discretionary hours remaining: between roughly 0 and 39.** Central estimate ≈ **15–20 hours.**

> **This is the single most important number in the analysis. He does not have 120 spare hours.
> He has something closer to fifteen.** Therefore no recommendation in this report may take the
> form "additionally build X." Every recommendation must either (a) fit inside ~15–20 hours, or
> (b) **change the shape of work already committed** so the same hours yield a second, better
> proof object. Anything else is a plan that fails in week three and takes the committed slate
> down with it.

### The largest available hour arbitrage [V-supported]
The most expensive committed line is *learning a professional NLE from zero* (25–35 hrs), and it
exists to serve the lane that fails its gates below.

- **CapCut:** first video in **30–60 minutes**; basic courses **5–10 hours**; intermediate
  **20–40 hours.** [V, miracamp.com/learn/capcut/the-best-courses ; benclaremont.com/capcut ; udemy CapCut beginner]
- **Resolve:** afternoon for a basic cut and export, but **1–2 weeks for basics** and weeks more
  for Colour/Fairlight comfort. [V, as cited above]
- **And CapCut is the tool named in Lane 3's postings** — "top skills: **AI Tools, CapCut,
  video editing**" [V, phase-2 §3]. Resolve is named in Lane 1's postings, which fail G1/G2/G3.

**Arbitrage: finish the master cut at a high standard in CapCut (with Resolve deferred to
Q1 2027), and ~15–25 hours are released without losing a single committed deliverable.** Those
released hours are exactly the hours the winning lane needs. Sound design and grading are
achievable to a portfolio standard in CapCut for a 45–60s piece; a colourist would know the
difference and a screener at a DTC brand will not. If he wants Resolve for its own sake, that is
a legitimate personal choice — but it should be made knowing it costs the top-ranked lane its
entry ticket this window.

## 4. Lane 4 — Creative strategist (performance / growth sub-lane)

### E5 — remaining gap at mid-November, costed
| Gap | Cost | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Hands-on Meta Ads Manager** | **8–12 hrs** [M] | Account setup, pixel/events basics, campaign structure, reading breakdowns |

| Direct-response copy fundamentals | **absorbed** into the above | Reading + writing the hooks he tests |
| **Total** | **43–64 hrs, ~$450–600 cash** | Well inside the 120-hour gate — **but above the ~15–20 discretionary hours**, which is why §0's arbitrage is load-bearing, not optional |

### The two structural moves that make the hours work
1. **Re-shape the committed client pilot.** Instead of "make me a 30-second video for $300–750,"
   sell **"I make the creative and run it as ads on your account, and give you a one-page report
   on what worked."** Same production hours, +3–5 hrs of reporting, and it converts a favour
   into the lane's exact proof object — **including the "account access for audit" evidence that
   the recruiting literature names as a most-reliable evaluation method** [V]. It also raises the
   price defensibly, because he is now selling performance, not files.
2. **Run D.R.C.A. as real paid ads with his own money.** Directly blessed by the hiring side:
   **"Run real campaigns with your own money or for a friend's business before you apply —
   agencies hire juniors who can already read an ad account, not ones who need six months of
   training to be useful."** [V, adage.com/agencies/aa-how-to-land-a-junior-job-what-leaders-say-works/]

### What his $150/month can and cannot buy — stated honestly
| Metric class | Achievable at ~$150/mo (~$450 over the window)? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| CPM, CTR, **hook rate** (3s views / impressions), thumbstop, engagement, CPC | **Yes.** "Quick signal test requires **$50–100 per creative**" → **4–6 creatives** at signal level over 90 days | [V, extuitive.com/articles/meta-ads-minimum-budget-for-testing ; thesocialoutline.com/blog/creative-testing-budget-meta-ads] |
| **ROAS / CPA / conversion-validated results** | **No.** Valid conversion test **$200–500 per creative**; rigorous A/B **$500–1,000 per variant**; statistically significant Meta data needs **$1,500–3,000/month**; a $10/day budget sits in "Learning Limited" with CPA ~3× expected | [V, same + stackmatix.com/blog/facebook-ads-minimum-budget-requirements ; roaspig.com/blog/minimum-viable-budget-testing-facebook-ads/] |
| Timeline | Low-budget campaigns need **4–6 weeks minimum**, ≥14 days before any decision — so **start in week 1, not week 8** | [V, same] |

**So the honest claim he can make is upper-funnel creative signal, not ROAS.** He must say so
explicitly in the teardowns — "$120 spend, engagement objective, hook rate 31% vs 19% on the
control, not conversion-validated" — because a strategist who overstates their data is
disqualified by exactly the "surface-level operator" filter [V] this lane is now screening for.
Stating the limitation *is* the credibility. And the client pilot's account is where a real
conversion metric can appear, on someone else's budget, legitimately.

### Gates

  Paid Social [V]; Atomic HR Creative Strategist, Paid Social, Remote US [V]; **Upwork Creative
  Strategist DTC Performance Ads, Part-Time → Full-Time, contract-to-hire, posted 2026-06-10**
  [V]; ForceBrands Performance Creative Strategist for DTC Growth [V]; plus 91 NYC "junior
  strategist" postings [C, Jan 2026] and 88 remote Meta-ads-specialist postings [C, Jul 2026].
  Documented non-posting channels with named venues: Thrive Capital portfolio board, ConstantHire,
  MarketerHire, 3Search, eCommercePlacement [V].
- **G2 — PASS.** 43–64 hours + ~$450–600, versus a 120-hour allowance. Achievable **only** with
  §0's arbitrage and §4's two re-shaping moves. Without them it collides with committed work,
  and that collision is the main execution risk in this report.
- **G3 — PASS, most strongly of any lane.** His weapon (paid professional ad-revenue economics

## 8. Contradictions with earlier phases, stated as required
- **Phase 1 → Phase 4:** Phase 1 ranked Lane 3 first on posting supply. Phase 4 ranks it second.
  Supply is not the binding constraint at 242 applications per opening; screen-survivability at
  low hour-cost is, and Lane 4 wins that on a documented competitive gap plus roughly double
  the comp.
- **Phase 2 → Phase 4:** Phase 2 treated the 120-hour gate as the operative budget. Phase 4
  finds the real discretionary budget is **~15–20 hours**, which changes the gate from "what can
  he add" to "what can he re-shape." Every surviving recommendation was rewritten under that
  constraint.
- **The candidate's own plan → Phase 4:** the committed slate is built to serve Lane 1, the lane
  that fails three of four gates. It is not wasted — its outputs feed Lanes 3 and 4 once
  re-shaped and re-framed — but its *tool choice* (Resolve) and its *framing* (art-first
  portfolio) are both optimised for the wrong door.
