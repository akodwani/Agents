## Tier definitions (derived from P1, not generic)
P1 says the four sub-$90 rows all sat at 36–41% thumbstop, and rows both below *and above* that band missed target. So tiers score **right-person attention**, not volume:

- **A** — predicted in-band. Stops someone with this problem; slides past everyone else.
- **B** — in-band but under-differentiated, or predicted just below.
- **C** — predicted out-of-band. Either invisible (C03/C06 mode) or broad-appeal bait that recruits non-buyers (C04/C10 mode). **A C is not a weak hook. It's often the most "engaging" one.**

Constraints applied throughout: no banned phrase from Pass 3 §2 (incl. "rambling" — AudioPen's word); numbers are the speaker's own, never borrowed; pain or transformation visible by 2s; solo staging (WS-3).

## 2. The kills

**All three Cs, first.** #25 is a slogan any competitor could run unchanged. #27 and #28 both argue with the category out loud — #28 is a verbal trust claim, which is precisely the P2 failure that took C06 to $333.33. WS-3 stays in the scripts as **staging**, never as a line. That was the plan in Pass 3 §3 and the bank confirms it: every attempt to *say* the solo position produced a bad hook.

**Then the near-duplicates.** #18 is #14 narrower. #30 is #29 in past tense — and past tense is the tell for P2 violations: reporting what happened instead of showing it happen. #8 and #9 describe a contrast instead of putting it on screen; C02 shows what that costs ($91.43, 0.9% CTR).

**#23 is the painful one.** "I remember deciding. I don't remember what." is the best sentence in the bank. It's cut because (a) it has no 0s visual — a blank doc is nothing to stop for, and P1 says invisible is the worse of the two failure modes ($225, $333), and (b) it sits in Cluster 3, the cluster Pass 2 could not evidence. Best line, weakest support. It survives as an **alternate hook** in Script C so it's cheap to test.

**#10 and #7 are cut for C04 risk.** Both are charming. Charm is what stopped 45% of scrollers and converted 14% of them. Anything whose payoff is a laugh is out.

**#16 and #17** are cut for a vague 0s payoff and for Cluster 3 exposure respectively.
