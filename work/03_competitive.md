## 3. Three white-space angles

Each has to clear two bars: Pass 1's data says the mechanism converts, and the category isn't occupying it. **Bar two is reasoned from documented positioning, not observed in live ads** — see §0.

### WS-1 — Show the ugly input
Every player in the category shows clean output. Their brand incentive is to look polished; AudioPen sells "polished text", Otter sells the tidy archive `[retrieved]`. **Nobody puts the messy raw thing on screen**, because it's off-brand.

That's the opening, and Pass 1 says it's also the money. P3: C05 and C07 are the only explicitly two-state ads in the account and they're #1 and #2 on CPA; C02 shows only the clean end state and posts the worst CTR in the test at $91.43. `[brief]` **The mess is the hook. The category has ceded it.**

It also answers the #1 objection — accuracy — *structurally*. Put the real transcript next to the real output and the viewer grades it themselves. That's a proof device, not a claim, which is what P2 demands.

### WS-2 — Sell the backlog, not the next note
Everyone advertises capture: record the next thing, join the next call `[retrieved]`. Nobody advertises to **the pile you already have and have never played back.**

C09 did exactly this — "I imported 120 voice notes and found 18 tasks" — and posted **41% thumbstop, the highest of any winner, at $82.09 on the largest budget in the test.** `[brief]` It's also the cleanest fit with P4: the number is the user's own, generated on screen, needs no citation.

Strategically it's a better sale: capture asks for a new habit, backlog asks for one afternoon and pays off immediately.

### WS-3 — The solo recorder
The category's loudest trust problem is a bot in a call recording people who didn't consent `[retrieved]`. LoopNote has no bot and no second party. **Structurally immune, never once claimed.**

The trap: saying "we don't record your colleagues" is a defensive verbal claim and violates P2. **So don't say it — set it.** Every script stages a solo moment: a car, a stairwell, a walk to the train, a desk after everyone's left. The viewer never thinks about consent because there's visibly nobody there. Category baggage sheds without a word spent on it.

`[assumption]` on the causal claim that this shifts trust — it's unmeasured. What's not assumption is that it costs nothing: the solo moments are the ones the pain clusters already live in (Pass 2 §3).
