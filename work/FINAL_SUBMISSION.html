What the data says

The account is at $105.12 per paid start against a $90 target, and six of ten tests missed. The useful finding isn't which ones lost — it's that the four ads with the best attention and the four with the best trial→paid conversion share zero rows. C10 posted the best thumbstop in the test (50%) and the worst conversion (11.5%, $190 CPA). C05 posted 39% and converted at 21.0% for $61.97. Attention isn't the constraint; it's being bought from the wrong people. The mechanism underneath is that the ad sets the expectation the trial has to satisfy — the skit promised a joke and delivered software (14.0% paid), the demo promised exactly what the app does (21.0%), the before/after promised a transformation (26.8%, best in the test). Which yields one rule: show the transformation, don't describe it. The five ads where the product's output is on screen blended $76.61; the five where someone talks about it blended $192.71 — at nearly identical average thumbstop (36% vs 34%). The entire difference happens after the click.

Two supporting reads. C03 is not a message failure — the founder talking head converts trial→paid at 22.2%, fourth-best of ten, on the worst thumbstop (18%) in the test. Its argument works; its container is invisible. And TikTok is bimodal, not weak: it holds the best row in the account (C05, $61.97) and three of the five worst. It punishes entertainment framing and rewards demo framing harder than Meta does.

Every script below opens on the mess rather than the clean version, and closes on a number that belongs to the person holding the phone rather than an industry statistic.

What I'd test next, and how I'd kill

  1. Script C's hook, dread-first vs. proof-first. Same body, same CTA, two openings. The cleanest read available on whether this audience is moved by the cost or by the relief.
  2. Re-house C03's argument. It converts at 22.2% inside a container nobody watches. The founder's actual reason for building this, spoken over a screen recording instead of into a lens. Cheap test, and the data says the message is already good.
  3. Deliberately break the attention band. Every winner sat at 36–41% thumbstop. Run one hook engineered to exceed 45% and confirm it converts worse. If it doesn't, my central claim is wrong and I'd rather find out for $500 than build a quarter on it.
  4. Kill rule: anything over $90 CPA at $500 spent, no exceptions and no "give it another day." Scale rule: whatever holds under $80 at $1,500 gets the budget from the six over-target rows. Reallocating that $21,700 at the winners' blended $74.62 takes the account from 391 paid starts to roughly 551 — from 17% over target to 17% under — with no change to spend, offer, or landing page.

AI usage

Required disclosure, and I'd rather over-report it.

I ran this inside Claude Code (Anthropic), an agentic terminal harness, as a structured multi-pass process — data forensics, research, hook generation, drafting, adversarial critique, revision. Specifically:

  • All arithmetic — CPA, trial→paid, cohort splits, correlations — computed in Python it wrote and I checked, not estimated. The brief's PDF was also parsed programmatically because the container had no PDF tooling.
  • Research — web search for category positioning and the AI-notetaker trust landscape. See the limitation below.
  • Drafting and hook generation — 30 hooks generated and cut to 8.
  • Adversarial critique — I had it score the scripts as a hostile evaluator and it found three real defects, including cutting the best line I had written ("I wasn't behind on work. I was behind on people.") for explaining what the screen had already shown.
  • The mock LoopNote UI — built as HTML and rendered to PNG, with the hook's "31" count verified by an assertion rather than by eye.

The judgement calls — which rows matter, what the pattern means, what to cut — are mine.

One limitation worth stating. I wanted verbatim user language from Reddit and App Store reviews. Reddit blocks the crawler, and the App Store, G2 and the Meta Ad Library were all blocked by the sandbox's network policy. So I did not log a single live competitor ad, and there are no scraped user quotes in this submission — I chose to say so rather than generate plausible ones. What I used instead was the account's own tested copy, which is arguably the better corpus: it's language that was actually put in front of this audience with thumbstop and paid conversion attached. C04's "your boss asks what was decided in the meeting" hit 45% thumbstop — the pain is validated; only the comedic framing failed it. Script C reclaims that moment straight.

Also disclosed: the produced version uses a mock LoopNote interface I built. The memo, the word count and the 31 are real; the app UI is a prop. An ad whose argument is "judge the evidence yourself" shouldn't be quiet about which part is a prop.

Bonus

Script A is production-ready — mock screens built and rendered, shot list, phone settings, VO direction, edit order, export specs for 9:16 and 4:5. No talent, no location, no lighting: a screen recording and a phone mic in a parked car, about an hour.
