# PASS 5 — Scripts v1

**Brief re-read.** The line driving a convention across all three: *"The best ads are also direct and don't try to masquerade as something they're not."*

So every script **says it's an ad, out loud, in the last four seconds** — and pairs that admission with the one thing that isn't sponsored: the memo, the number, the hallway are real. *"Paid ad. Real memo."* Disclosure and proof in the same breath. It's the most direct reading of the brief, it's the opposite of fake-organic, and it makes the proof device stronger rather than weaker.

Format families follow Pass 1 §8. **Script C is a deliberate port of C07 from YouTube Shorts to Meta/TikTok** — flagged per Pass 0 §6.1.

# SCRIPT A — "Thirty-one uhhs"

**Platform:** TikTok-first (9:16). **Target length:** 24s. **Persona:** founder / operator.
**Pain cluster:** 2 (voice-memo graveyard). **White space:** WS-1 (show the ugly input).
**Lineage:** C05 — screen-record demo, $61.97 CPA, best row in the test — with an own-number hook grafted on from C09 (41% TS).
**Principles:** P2 (product does the work on screen, no describing) · P3 (mess first, clean output second) · P4 (own number, on screen) · P7 (demo format is what TikTok rewards — C05 is TikTok's best row).

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 0:07–0:10 | Still scrolling. Scrollbar shows how much is left. | "I've never played one of these back. Not once." | — | Room tone. |
| 0:10–0:12 | Thumb taps once. Brief processing state. | *(silence)* | — | Single soft UI tick. Music enters here, low. |
| 0:12–0:18 | Task list resolves. Three items, two with names attached. Camera holds — long enough to actually read them. | "Three tasks. Two with names on them. One I'd completely forgotten about." | — | Music low. |

**Hook + alternates**
- **Lead (#3):** "My transcript says 'uhh' thirty-one times."
- **Alt 1 (#5):** "Read that and tell me a computer could do anything with it." — opens on the full-screen transcript, invites the accuracy objection in.
- **Alt 2 (#1):** "This is four minutes of me talking to myself in a parking garage." — softer, sets solo staging.

**Proof device.** A continuous, un-cut screen recording. The transcript is genuinely ugly and stays legible long enough to read. At 0:18 the raw input and its output sit side by side and the viewer grades the extraction themselves.

**CTA.** *"Link's in the caption."* at **0:22**, over the LoopNote mark, immediately after the disclosure. One CTA. No "sign up today", no urgency device.

**Objection pre-empted.** Accuracy (Pass 2 §4, objection #1) — answered structurally, not verbally. Nobody says "it's accurate." The viewer reads the input and the output and decides. Per P2, saying it would be the failure.

**Production notes.** Phone screen recording + phone mic, shot in a car. **LoopNote stand-in:** mock UI (Pass 9) — a transcript screen and a task screen; the tap between them is the only interaction needed. No talent, no lighting, no location fee. Props: phone, car.

**Prediction.** Thumbstop **37–40%** — in-band (P1); an own-number is specific enough to stop the right person and boring enough to be skipped by everyone else. Hold ~27%, matching C05. Trial→paid **22–25%**, above C05's 21.0% because the accuracy proof at 0:18 does work C05's hook didn't. **CPA at or slightly under C05's $61.97.** Highest-confidence script of the three.

**Proof device.** Two of them. The memo list at 0:00 is a real phone's real backlog. The extraction at 0:15–0:24 shows counts the viewer can see resolve. The turn at 0:24 — *work* → *people* — is the persuasive beat, and it earns its place because the screen already proved the claim.

**CTA.** *"Trial link's below."* at **0:29**, after the disclosure.

**Objection pre-empted.** Accuracy again, plus a second: *"I'd have to change how I work."* Answered by the premise — this uses memos that already exist. No new habit, one Sunday. That's WS-2's strategic advantage doing double duty (Pass 3 §3).

**Production notes.** Phone, kitchen table, window light, no lighting kit. The 63 must be a real count on a real device — if it isn't, change the number to whatever is.

**Prediction.** Thumbstop **39–42%**, in-band, tracking C09's 41%. Trial→paid **22–24%**. **CPA $72–82**, at or slightly below C09's $82.09. Longest of the three and the one most exposed on length — first thing I'd cut in Pass 6.

# SCRIPT C — "Forty seconds"

**Platform:** both (9:16). **Target length:** 20s. **Persona:** operator.
**Pain cluster:** 1 (meeting aftermath — the most validated pain in the dataset).
**Lineage:** **C07 before/after — 26.8% trial→paid, best conversion in the test — ported from YT Shorts to Meta/TikTok.** Plus C04's moment (45% thumbstop) played straight instead of as a bit.
**Principles:** P1 (stop the right person) · P3 (two-state) · P6 (C04's pain is validated; only the comedic framing failed) · P5 (built for conversion, not clicks).

| Time | On screen | VO / dialogue | On-screen text | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Office corridor. Phone up. Calendar notification visible in the corner: next meeting, 3 minutes. Walking. | "That call ended forty seconds ago." | — | Real corridor noise. No music. |
| 0:03–0:06 | Still walking. Slightly out of breath. | "Two of the four things are already gone." | — | Corridor noise. |
| 0:06–0:12 | Stops. Taps record. Talks fast, overlapping, unpolished — this is the mess, live. | *(to phone)* "Priya needs the deck Thursday — I said I'd get the pricing thing to Marcus, uh, and someone's chasing the vendor —" | — | Raw. Voice only. |
| 0:12–0:16 | Screen. Tasks resolving. Four items. Camera holds long enough to read all four. | *(silence)* | — | Single UI tick. Music enters. |
| 0:16–0:18 | Task list held. | "I don't need a summary. I need the things I owe people." | — | Music. |
| 0:18–0:20 | LoopNote mark. | "LoopNote. Paid ad, real hallway. **Link below.**" | `Paid ad.` | Music out. |

**Hook + alternates**
- **Lead (#19):** "That call ended forty seconds ago." → "Two of the four things are already gone."
- **Alt 1 (#21):** "Here are the four things, thirty seconds after the call." — **proof-first instead of dread-first. This is a planned A/B, not a backup** (Pass 4 checkpoint).
- **Alt 2 (#23):** "I remember deciding. I don't remember what." — best line in the bank, weakest 0s visual; cheap to test here.

**Proof device.** The mess is generated live, on camera, in one take. The viewer hears the actual disorganised sentence at 0:06 and reads the actual four tasks at 0:12. Nothing between them is cut — the two-state contrast is witnessed rather than asserted, which is C07's whole mechanism.

**CTA.** *"Link below."* at **0:19**.

**Objection pre-empted.** Accuracy — the strongest version of it in the three scripts, because input and output are separated by six seconds of unbroken time. Also the bot-consent objection, structurally: an empty corridor, one person, no call in progress, nothing recording anybody else (WS-3, staged not stated).

**Production notes.** One phone, one corridor, one take. Second unit: the screen recording. Best shot at the end of a workday when the corridor's empty. **Stand-in:** mock LoopNote task screen. The four tasks in the mock must match what's actually said at 0:06 — if the take changes, the mock changes.

**Prediction.** Thumbstop **35–38%**, low end of band — dread-first is more selective than an own-number. Hold ~24%. **Trial→paid the highest of the three, 25–27%**, tracking C07's 26.8%: two-state contrast is the mechanism most associated with conversion in this account. **CPA $68–78.** Shortest, so the cheapest to serve and the most forgiving on frequency.
