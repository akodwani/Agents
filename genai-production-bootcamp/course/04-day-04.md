# DAY 4 — Premiere Fundamentals and Pacing

**TIME BUDGET: 5h** — 1h watch · 0.5h read · 3h do · 0.5h QC

## OUTCOME
A configured, disciplined Premiere project and the three-pacing-styles exercise complete.

## WHY THIS MATTERS
18 hard requirements in the market sample. But more practically: pacing is the skill that makes
six generated clips feel like one film. It is also the cheapest quality gain available to you —
no credits, no GPU, pure judgement.

## LEARN
- Project/bin/sequence hygiene; **24fps decided once, never mixed**
- Proxies (Premiere 26 has AI proxies for weak hardware — use them)
- Source/record workflow; ripple / roll / slip / slide
- **J-cuts and L-cuts** — the highest-value edit technique for AI film
- Pacing as a deliberate instrument
- Colour management basics: why generated footage needs matching before grading

## WATCH
Your audited **Premiere PRIMARY**. If `pq-O1VgEj_4` survived audit, watch **only the project
setup and trim-tool chapters (~30 min)** — its short-form focus is useful for your reel cut
later, not for narrative editing.
Plus ~20 min on J/L cuts specifically, from whichever source scored highest.

## READ
- `modules/premiere.md` §1–§2 — 20 min
- Premiere 26 release notes on Media Intelligence and Generative Extend — 10 min

## DO
1. **(30 min) Project setup.** Bins, 24fps sequence, proxy workflow enabled. Label colours for
   shot state (red = regenerate, yellow = needs comp, green = final).
2. **(150 min) Exercise P1 — three pacing styles.** Take your Day 1 and Day 3 generations
   (15–20 seconds of raw material) and cut them three ways:
   - **Tense** — long holds, few cuts, sound-led, one late payoff cut
   - **Kinetic** — short shots, cuts on action, escalating rhythm
   - **Classical** — establishing → coverage → resolution, J/L cuts throughout
   **All three must be the same total length.**
3. **(30 min)** Colour-match pass: put your best shot on the timeline as reference, use Lumetri
   Comparison view, match black point → white point → hue on the others. Check the Waveform.
4. **(30 min)** Export all three. Write one paragraph naming which cut does the work in each.

## VERIFY
- [ ] All three cuts are the same duration
- [ ] A viewer can tell them apart without being told what you did
- [ ] ≥2 J-cuts or L-cuts in the Classical version
- [ ] Colour matched — shots read as one camera
- [ ] Blacks are off 0 and whites off 100 on the Waveform unless deliberately clipped

## SHIP
`day04/pacing_tense.mp4`, `pacing_kinetic.mp4`, `pacing_classical.mp4`, `pacing_notes.md`.

## FAILURE CONDITIONS
- **All three feel the same** → you changed shot *order*, not shot *length* and cut *placement*.
  Pacing is duration and cut point, not sequence.
- **Timeline stutters** → mixed frame rates. Check every clip's interpreted rate. This is the
  most common Premiere problem with generated footage and it will recur all course.
- **Banding in dark areas** → enable *Render at Maximum Depth* on export.

## RECOVERY
Not enough footage for three cuts: generate two more 4-second clips at 480p fast tier. Do not
spend credits on quality here — you are practising editorial judgement.

## STOP RULE
Stop when someone else can watch the three cuts and correctly identify which is which.
