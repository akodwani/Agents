# DAY 6 — After Effects Core

**TIME BUDGET: 5h** — 1.5h watch · 0.5h read · 2.5h do · 0.5h QC

## OUTCOME
AE fundamentals under control, and **AE1 complete**: an obvious AI artifact repaired invisibly.

## WHY THIS MATTERS
22 hard requirements. But the deeper reason: AE is what makes generated footage *shippable*.
Agencies in particular do not believe a reel that has never been through a comp. This is the
second gate after ComfyUI.

## LEARN
- Comps, frame rate discipline, pre-comps, adjustment layers, render queue
- Keyframes and **the graph editor** — nothing in a finished comp has linear easing
- Masks: bezier, feather, expansion, animation
- **Track mattes** (alpha, luma, and both inverted) and blending modes
- **The order of operations** — repair upstream first, grain last

## WATCH
Your audited **After Effects PRIMARY** — foundations chapters, ~60 min.
⚠️ **VERSION WARNING: AE 26.2 replaced Roto Brush with the Object Matte tool.** If your primary
teaches Roto Brush, watch it for *matte judgement* (edge quality, spill, holdout) and ignore the
click path entirely. Verify every tool location against your own 26.x UI before following along.
Plus ~20 min specifically on the graph editor if your primary skims it.

## READ
- `modules/after-effects.md` §1–§2 — 20 min
- Adobe Object Matte help page — 10 min

## DO
1. **(20 min)** Project setup. Comp at 24fps matching your master resolution. Learn where the
   graph editor is and set easy-ease as a habit.
2. **(30 min) Graph editor drill.** Animate one solid across frame three ways: linear, ease-in-out,
   and a custom curve with a snap. Watch them back-to-back. **This is the difference between
   amateur and professional motion and it takes 30 minutes to learn.**
3. **(20 min) Matte drill.** Build a track-matte reveal. Do it with alpha, then luma, then
   inverted. Know which you'd reach for and why.
4. **(120 min) EXERCISE AE1 — repair an obvious AI artifact.**
   - **First: run the upstream repair.** `video_deflicker` → `bytedance_video_upscale(preset="aigc")`.
     Compare before/after. *Never grind in AE what a 20-cent API call fixes.*
   - Then in AE: stabilise if needed → patch or clone the remaining artifact → track the patch →
     match grain over the top.
5. **(30 min)** Export a before/after split-screen. This goes straight into your portfolio.

## VERIFY
- [ ] The artifact is invisible at 100% playback speed
- [ ] The patch does not slide — scrub frame by frame across the whole shot
- [ ] Grain matches the plate (sample it, don't guess)
- [ ] You ran the upstream repair chain *first* and can show what it bought you
- [ ] Before/after split-screen exported

## SHIP
`day06/ae1_before_after.mp4`, the AE project, `day06/repair_notes.md` recording what the upstream
chain fixed vs what AE had to fix.

## FAILURE CONDITIONS
- **Patch slides** → your track failed. Try Mocha AE planar tracking instead of point tracking;
  on flat-ish surfaces Mocha is right far more often than beginners assume.
- **Grain looks wrong** → you added grain to one layer instead of over the whole comp. Grain is
  a property of the final image. Adjustment layer, on top, always.
- **You spent 2 hours on an artifact that should have been regenerated** → see the AE module's
  "can and cannot fix" table. Comp fixes surfaces, not physics.

## RECOVERY
If the artifact is anatomy, physical impossibility or object permanence: **stop.** Those are
unfixable in comp. Pick a different clip for this exercise and log the finding.

## STOP RULE
Stop when AE1 passes and you can name three things the graph editor changed about your animation.
