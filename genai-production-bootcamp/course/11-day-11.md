# DAY 11 — Blender Previs Rig

**TIME BUDGET: 5h** — 1h watch · 0.5h read · 3h do · 0.5h QC

## OUTCOME
A working grey-box previs scene with three cine cameras respecting one 180° line, and clay
renders from each.

## WHY THIS MATTERS
This module rose in value during the research for this course. The live Higgsfield catalog shows
`video_references` as a first-class input on six video models plus explicit motion transfer via
Genjutsu — which means a grey-box Blender animation is not a vague influence on your output, it
*is* the camera move. Previs stopped being a detour and became your strongest control primitive.

## LEARN
- Blender only as a previs instrument — no modelling, no shading, no sculpting
- Cameras: focal length, **sensor width** (without it "50mm" is meaningless), position, aim
- Grey-box blocking: hero, foreground occluder, background geometry
- Why the geometry must be *correct*, not attractive

## WATCH
Your audited **Blender PRIMARY** — likely **Andrew Price, "How to control AI with Blender"**
(`1vB3JXzewx0`). ~45 min. This was flagged as the highest-priority candidate in the whole course
because it covers exactly the previs→AI bridge.
Plus the camera-rig and animation chapters only of `g_YOqMaoIyE` (Blender 5.1 camera tutorial),
~15 min. **Skip its lighting and look-dev sections entirely.**

## READ
- `modules/blender-ai-previs.md` §1–§3 — 20 min
- Skim `tools/blender/previs_rig.py` and its comments — 10 min

## DO
1. **(45 min)** Install Blender. Run `tools/blender/previs_rig.py` in the Scripting tab.
   Confirm it produces `previs_out/clay/`. **If it errors, read the error — it is written with
   version fallbacks but it has not been executed on your machine (see `research/source_audit.md`).**
2. **(90 min) Adapt it to YOUR scene.** Change the geometry to match the environment you want
   for the final film. Keep the structure: one hero, one foreground occluder, background
   geometry. Keep all three cameras on the negative-Y side of the line.
3. **(45 min) Camera drill.** Render the same subject at 24mm, 50mm and 85mm from positions that
   frame the subject comparably. **Understand viscerally what focal length does to background
   compression** — this is the lesson.
4. **(30 min)** Sketch the floor plan. Draw the 180° line. Mark all three cameras. Confirm on
   paper that none crosses.

## VERIFY
- [ ] Script runs, clay renders produced from all three cameras
- [ ] All three cameras on the same side of the 180° line — verified on the floor-plan sketch
- [ ] The 85mm reads as a genuine close-up, not a cropped medium
- [ ] The foreground occluder is clearly visible and creates real parallax
- [ ] Scene is saved and the `.py` is committed

## SHIP
`04_previs/blender/previs_rig.py` (your adapted version), `04_previs/clay/` (3 renders),
floor-plan sketch.

## FAILURE CONDITIONS
- **Script errors on your Blender version** → most likely the render engine name or a compositor
  node name. The script has fallbacks for the engine; for node names, check Blender's own Python
  tooltips (enable Developer Extras → hover any field → see its Python path).
- **Cameras all look the same** → you changed focal length without changing distance. To compare
  lenses honestly, reposition so the subject occupies a similar frame proportion.
- **You start modelling** → stop. Grey boxes are the deliverable. If you catch yourself adding
  a bevel, you have misunderstood the module.

## RECOVERY
If Blender is genuinely fighting you, run headless:
`blender --background --python tools/blender/previs_rig.py`. It removes the entire UI as a
variable and the errors get much clearer.

## STOP RULE
Stop when three clay renders exist from three cameras on one side of the line. **Not** when the
scene looks good. It should look like grey boxes. That is correct.
