# DAY 15 — Lock the Film

**TIME BUDGET: 5h** — 0h watch · 0.5h read · 4h do · 0.5h QC (**GATE G1**)

## OUTCOME
The final film is chosen, structured and specified. Shot list complete. Floor plan drawn.
Nothing is generated today, and that is deliberate.

## WHY THIS MATTERS
Phase 4 is 35 hours and it is the tightest part of the course. Every hour of specification today
saves roughly three hours of regeneration later. **Gate G2 (tomorrow) is the highest-value gate
in the course** — fixing geography on a board costs 20 minutes; fixing it after generation costs
a day and 40 generations.

## LEARN
- Choosing scope honestly rather than ambitiously
- Structuring escalation across 6–10 shots
- Designing shots around your model's known weaknesses

## WATCH / READ
`projects/final_project_brief.md` in full — 25 min. Read all three candidate briefs.

## DO
1. **(45 min) Choose your brief.** A "The Signal" (easiest — static hero, minimal contact
   physics), B "The Intruder" (medium — creature locomotion is your biggest QC risk), or C
   "The Pursuit" (hardest — sustained screen direction, strongest portfolio piece if landed).
   **Choose honestly. A completed Brief A beats an abandoned Brief C.**
   Write down *why* you chose it, in terms of the failure modes you have actually hit in
   Days 1–14. That is evidence-based scoping.
2. **(45 min) Beats.** 4–6 sentences. Each must be a *change of state*, not a description.
   Mark where the escalation turns and where the payoff lands.
3. **(90 min) Shot list.** 6–10 rows into the Console, every column populated. For each shot
   decide and record: **control rung, model, and which technique requirement it satisfies.**
   Explicitly assign:
   - which 2 shots are the **difficult motion shots**
   - which 1 uses **3D/previs control**
   - which 1 uses **ComfyUI structural control**
   - which 1 gets the **substantial AE composite**
   - which 2 are **locked off**
4. **(45 min) Floor plan.** Draw the location. Mark every camera position. **Draw the 180° line.**
   Verify no camera crosses it. Mark screen direction per shot.
5. **(30 min) Lens progression.** Write the focal length per shot and one sentence on what the
   progression does to the audience. If you cannot write that sentence, the progression is random.

## VERIFY — GATE G1 (STORY)
- [ ] The story can be told in 6–10 shots. If it needs 12, it is too big — cut it now
- [ ] Beginning, escalation and payoff are identifiable as specific shots
- [ ] Every technique requirement is assigned to a specific shot
- [ ] Floor plan drawn, 180° line drawn, no camera crosses it
- [ ] Lens progression is deliberate and you can defend it in one sentence
- [ ] **Read the beats aloud to someone. Can they tell you what happens?**

**G1 FAILS → do not proceed to Day 16.** Re-scope today. A day spent fixing the story is the
cheapest day in Phase 4.

## SHIP
`01_dev/final_beats.md`, `01_dev/final_shotlist.csv` (in the Console), floor-plan sketch,
`01_dev/lens_progression.md`, `01_dev/brief_choice_rationale.md`.

## FAILURE CONDITIONS
- **The story needs 12+ shots** → it is too big. Cut a beat, not a shot. Cutting shots from a
  too-big story produces an incoherent film; cutting a beat produces a smaller coherent one.
- **You cannot assign the technique requirements to specific shots** → you have not designed for
  them. Redesign a shot around each requirement now, cheaply, on paper.
- **You picked Brief C because it sounds impressive** → re-read your Day 5 and Day 10 failure
  logs and choose again on evidence.

## RECOVERY
If nothing is landing: take your Day 10 micro-scene and *extend* it into a full scene. Reusing
proven references and a proven location is a legitimate production decision, not a shortcut.

## STOP RULE
Stop when G1 passes and the shot list is complete. **Generating anything today is a mistake** —
it would anchor you to a shot before the story is locked.
