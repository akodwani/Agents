# DAY 2 — Storyboarding and Locked References

**TIME BUDGET: 5h** — 0.5h watch · 0.5h read · 3.5h do · 0.5h QC

## OUTCOME
An 8–12 frame coherent storyboard for a small scene, plus locked, versioned references for one
subject and one environment.

## WHY THIS MATTERS
This is the cheapest module in the course and the one with the largest effect on final quality.
It costs almost no credits and no GPU. A film with a good board and mediocre generations beats a
film with great generations and no board — every time, and visibly.

## LEARN
- Production boards vs pretty images (the distinction that matters)
- The pipeline: concept → beats → shot list → board → hero frames → locked refs
- Character-sheet construction and identity locking
- Reusable prompt structure (fixed slot order so prompts diff cleanly)

## WATCH
Your audited **Higgsfield PRIMARY** (likely `UdWYf6Ck2ZM` or `jazMJOMGStc`, Cinema Studio 2.5) —
**only the reference/Elements management chapters, ~25 min.**
⚠️ Platform is on **Cinema Studio 3.0**; these cover 2.5. Watch for *concepts* — how references
and Elements are organised — not for click paths. Skip all intro and hype segments.

## READ
- `modules/storyboarding.md` §2–§3 — 20 min
- Load the live character-sheet workflow: `get_workflow_instructions({workflow:"character-sheet"})` — 10 min

## DO
1. **(45 min) Exercise S2 — beats to shot list.** Pick a small scene idea (this is *practice*,
   not your final film). Write 4–6 beats, then a 6-row `shotlist.csv` with every column filled.
   Sketch the floor plan with camera positions and the 180° line.
2. **(60 min) Build the character sheet.** Run the `character-sheet` workflow for your subject.
   Front, 3/4, profile minimum. Register the result as a **Soul Cast** identity.
3. **(30 min) Lock the environment.** `soul_location` for one location. Generate it at three
   different lens/size combinations. **If it can't survive three angles, simplify the location.**
4. **(90 min) Exercise S3 — generate the board.** 8–12 frames with `nano_banana_pro`, carrying
   your locked refs as `image_references` on every frame.
5. **(30 min)** Version and freeze: `03_refs/char_hero_v1.png`, `env_*_v1.png`. Commit. Record
   SHA-256 of each locked ref in a `refs.json`.

## VERIFY
- [ ] Subject recognisably the same in every board frame
- [ ] Environment recognisably the same location
- [ ] Screen direction consistent across all frames
- [ ] ≥3 distinct shot sizes present
- [ ] **A stranger can describe the story from the boards in order, with no narration**
- [ ] Refs frozen, versioned and hashed

## SHIP
`02_boards/board_v1/` (8–12 frames), `03_refs/` (locked + hashed), `01_dev/shotlist.csv`,
floor-plan sketch.

## FAILURE CONDITIONS
- **Beautiful frames, no geography** → the classic failure. You made pretty images, not boards.
  Redo with the floor plan open beside you.
- **Subject drifts between frames** → your reference is not being applied. Confirm you are
  passing `image_references` on *every* call, not just the first.
- **You keep regenerating a "locked" reference** → then it isn't locked. Freeze it. If you must
  change it, increment to `v2` and consciously update everything downstream.

## RECOVERY
Subject drift persisting at rung 3: build a stronger character sheet with more views, and prefer
a subject with high-contrast distinctive features (markings, silhouette, colour) over a subtle one.

## STOP RULE
Stop when the stranger test passes. Not when the frames look good — when the **story reads**.
